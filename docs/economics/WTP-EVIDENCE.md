## Question set (to be used, not yet used)

Context

1. What are you building or operating that lets software spend money or commit to spend?
2. Who today decides what that software may spend, and how?
3. What happened the last time something spent more than intended? (Only if they volunteer it.)

Current alternatives 4. What do you use today to limit or audit that spending (provider limits, wallet limits, prompts, manual review, in-house code)? 5. What does that cost you in time or money, as best you can tell? 6. What do you not trust about it?

Value and budget 7. Who would own a budget for a control like this, and in which team? 8. How do you buy comparable infrastructure (usage, seats, annual contract)? 9. What unit would you expect to be charged by, and why? (Do not suggest one first.) 10. At what point would a price make you decide not to proceed? (Ask for a range only after they volunteer a unit.)

Constraints 11. What would security, procurement or legal require before you could use this? 12. What would make you stop using it?

Close 13. Who else should we speak to? 14. May we contact you again, and may we quote you (default: no)?

## Evidence standard

- Stated interest is not willingness to pay. Only a commitment (paid pilot, signed order) counts as strong evidence; anything else is weak.
- Prompted price answers are marked as such and weighted lower.
- A hypothesis in PRICING-ARCHITECTURE.md moves from "hypothesis" only when several independent entries support it.
